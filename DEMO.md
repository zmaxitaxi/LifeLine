# Two-minute live-weather demonstration

Start the complete application using [README](README.md). Open **Live weather forecast and tomorrow’s plan**. The location defaults to a representative Kisumu point; it is not a partner clinic. Keep the existing synthetic same-work comparison available as an offline backup.

| Time | Action | Explain |
|---|---|---|
| 0–20 s | Identify the clinic operator and select location. Fetch today/tomorrow. | A facility manager or energy technician needs a practical operating plan under limited solar and battery resources. Show actual source, local date, coordinates, timezone and retrieval time. Demand is illustrative/user-entered. |
| 20–40 s | Enter and confirm the assumed stored energy at tomorrow’s local midnight. Ask “Can our clinic safely operate tomorrow given the weather forecast?” | Midnight energy is an explicit assumption, not a prediction from today's current charge. The validated planning tool fetches the forecast and runs the physical model. The current guided assistant is deterministic; no hosted LLM is connected. |
| 40–70 s | Read the computed assessment and Before → After schedule. Expand the detailed comparison briefly. | Show which work moved, actual work completion, essential shortfall, reserve and losses. Compare existing, sunshine-first and MILP under identical inputs. A heuristic tie is disclosed; a battery gain achieved by deferring work is not a same-work improvement. |
| 70–95 s | Inspect the 50%-lower-sunshine row, or ask “What happens if tomorrow's sunshine is 50% lower?” | The model reruns with lower solar while keeping equipment, hardware and midnight battery fixed. Explain changes in reserve and completed work. Cases are independently reoptimized sensitivity tests, not probabilities or a fixed-schedule guarantee. |
| 95–110 s | Show an impossible configuration: in the engineering controls set solar to zero and assume zero midnight charge, then recalculate the forecast plan. | The weather can still be retrieved, but this configured system cannot supply its requirements. LifeLine reports the computed deficit; scheduling cannot create supply. Restore the clinic example afterward. |
| 110–120 s | Show the remaining limitation and next validation step. | Decision support only. Appliance cycles, staffing, battery condition, installation details and forecast errors need practitioner/engineering validation. No clinical-safety or electricity-availability guarantee. |

Use the actual live values. The recorded Kisumu November 14 forecast retrieved on November 13 at 08:34 UTC produced 7.132 kWh estimated solar and a 4.831 kWh optimized minimum battery with a 6 kWh midnight assumption and all 2.1 kWh of flexible work completed. With 50% lower sunshine, it produced 3.566 kWh solar, 4.038 kWh minimum battery, and 2.1 kWh flexible work deferred. Essential unmet demand was zero in both cases; the lower-sunshine case breached the reserve target. Those are dated model results, not measured clinic benefits or stable future demo constants. See [source/evidence](docs/evidence/live-forecast-run.json).

If the weather provider is unavailable, disclose the failure and deliberately choose **historical NASA weather** or **synthetic sunshine**. Keep a labeled recording as an optional presentation backup if one is made; none is claimed to have been recorded. Cached forecasts retain their original retrieval time and disclose stale status.

The original synthetic scheduling demo remains useful: both alternative schedules complete 2.1 kWh and retain 2.211 kWh more at midnight than the fixed baseline; sunshine-first matches MILP. High Demand and Capacity Explorer demonstrate that more storage alone does not resolve an output-power bottleneck. These are separate scenarios, not weather-only changes to the same clinic.
