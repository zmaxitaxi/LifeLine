# Two complementary computed demonstrations

Results are generated by the real simulator/MILP, not read from this document. Use the original equal-work case to demonstrate battery preservation and the competing-window case to demonstrate capacity-aware scheduling. Neither is measured clinic performance.

## Equal work, less battery use

The original `scheduling` example remains unchanged. Fixed, sunshine-first and optimized schedules each complete 2.1 kWh. Moving work into sunshine preserves **2.210526 kWh at midnight**, improves the full-day minimum by **0.368421 kWh**, and reduces conversion losses by **0.110526 kWh**. Essential demand is fully served. Sunshine-first matches the optimizer. Midnight energy, daily minimum and evening minimum are distinct metrics.

## Competing windows and limited output

In **Detailed mode**, choose **Load competing-window example**. The button fetches an input-only request and solves it afresh. The API is `GET /api/demos/competing-windows` followed by the unchanged `POST /api/simulate`.

Weather is the bundled NASA POWER Kisumu January 14, 2025 irradiation. Demand/hardware/windows are constructed assumptions: 1.5 kW PV, 8 kWh storage, 7 kWh starting energy, 4.8 kWh reserve and 0.4 kW battery output. Shared device charging requires 1.8 kWh at up to 0.6 kW during 08:00–21:00; a reserve-tank transfer requires 2 kWh at up to 1 kW during 11:00–15:00. All original essential equipment remains fixed. All three schedules receive identical inputs.

| Computed metric | Existing | Sunshine-first | LifeLine MILP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Requested flexible energy | 3.800000 kWh | 3.800000 kWh | 3.800000 kWh |
| Completed flexible energy | 2.769560 kWh | 3.469864 kWh | 3.800000 kWh |
| Deferred flexible energy | 1.030440 kWh | 0.330136 kWh | 0 kWh |
| Essential unmet energy | 0 kWh | 0 kWh | 0 kWh |
| Minimum stored energy | 5.774021 kWh | 5.774021 kWh | 5.774021 kWh |
| Reserve breach | None | None | None |
| Conversion losses | 0.305760 kWh | 0.260256 kWh | 0.172773 kWh |

The optimizer leaves room for the restricted task and moves charging to other allowed slots. This completes **1.030440 kWh more than the existing schedule** and **0.330136 kWh more than this sunshine-first implementation**, with essential demand and reserve protected. There is no minimum-battery gain in this case. Completion differs, so it must not be called an equal-work battery-saving comparison.

The mechanism is testable: increasing the battery output limit removes the heuristic's work deficit. Reversing the greedy task order also completes all 3.8 kWh. A deadline-aware heuristic can close this gap. The example establishes the usefulness of considering competing windows/output limits, not universal superiority of MILP.

Both flexible tasks are explicitly interruptible energy aggregates. Only postpone water transfer after a facility operator verifies adequate essential water stock; tank state, hydraulic output, starting surges and actual pumping cycles are not modeled. The numerical output is kWh of modeled work, not liters of water, device counts or patients treated. These assumed windows and ratings have not been observed at a clinic.
